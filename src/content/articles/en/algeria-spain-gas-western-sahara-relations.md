> Editorial update: research completed on 21 July 2026 at 14:20 (Africa/Casablanca).

Pedro Sanchez's 20 July 2026 visit to Algiers places Algeria-Spain relations back in a cautious normalisation phase. Algiers and Madrid are speaking again about political dialogue, economic cooperation and strategic partnership. Gas matters, but it is not the whole story: Spanish companies, investment, migration, the Mediterranean and the Western Sahara file all sit in the same diplomatic field.

The central point is not to confuse warmer diplomacy with the disappearance of disagreements. Spain maintains its known support for Morocco's autonomy proposal, while Algeria continues to defend a UN-framed solution based on self-determination. That gap does not prevent working channels from reopening, but it requires sober language.

## What the visit changes

The bilateral relationship is emerging from a tense sequence that began in 2022. Reopening dialogue does not mean every file is solved; it means both governments want a working framework again. For Algeria, the issue is to preserve diplomatic principles while reopening economic possibilities with a nearby European neighbour. For Spain, the issue is energy, industry and Mediterranean stability.

## Gas, economy and limits

Algerian gas matters to Spain, but the topic should not be flattened into a slogan. Medgaz gives the relationship strategic depth without making Algeria Europe's single answer. Volumes, contracts, prices and technical capacity all limit triumphalist readings. The better question is not who replaces whom, but how reliable flows can be stabilised in a volatile period.

That reading connects with our files on [Algerian gas](/en/articles/algerie-energie-souverainete-gaz), [economy](/en/economy), [Western Sahara](/en/western-sahara). Gas is a sovereignty instrument, but also a constraint: a strong sector gives leverage, while diversification remains necessary.

## Western Sahara: careful terms

On Western Sahara, this article uses neutral terminology. Positions are attributed: Madrid supports Morocco's autonomy proposal; Algiers defends self-determination in the UN framework; the Polisario Front claims independence. None of those positions should be turned into an unsourced editorial verdict.

## What to watch

The next three days will show whether the visit produces precise sector announcements: contracts, investment calendars, joint commissions, energy follow-up or diplomatic gestures. Without an operational document, the strongest conclusion is this: the political channel is reactivated, while the relationship remains shaped by energy, trade and Western Sahara.

## FAQ

### Have Algeria and Spain'solved their crisis?
They have reopened high-level dialogue, but available sources do not support saying every disagreement has been solved.

### Can Algerian gas replace all Russian gas in Europe?
No. It can support European diversification, especially around Spain and the Mediterranean, but it cannot replace all Russian volumes by itself.

### Is Western Sahara outside the relationship?
No. It remains sensitive; each actor's position needs attribution and careful wording.
