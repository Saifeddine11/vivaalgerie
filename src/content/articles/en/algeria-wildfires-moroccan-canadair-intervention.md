Several wilayas in the north and other reported areas have recorded active outbreaks, while the General Directorate of Civil Protection (DGPC) has published hourly reports showing mass extinctions and fires still in progress. In Annaba, the commune of Seraïdi concentrated a critical part of the crisis, with hospital evacuations and material damage reported.

## What happened

Since the start of summer 2026, Algeria has been facing a particularly dense fire season. Data relayed by Anadolu Agency and CGTN, citing Civil Protection, indicate **3,719 fires** recorded between May 1 and July 20, 2026, compared to **1,501** over the same period in 2025 — an increase close to 150%. Since the beginning of July, approximately **1460** fire outbreaks have been cited.

The weather combination has made the situation worse: prolonged heatwave, hot winds, dry vegetation, difficult-to-access terrain. In several wilayas, forest, scrub, brush, harvest and sometimes palm grove fires required simultaneous interventions. Preventive evacuations were reported in several localities (Barbacha in Béjaïa, Blida, Skikda, Bouira, Guelma, among others), according to previous reports from mid-July.

In Annaba, a major fire broke out on Tuesday July 21–22 in the forests of Seraïdi before progressing towards urbanized areas. Local and national authorities have reinforced the ground presence, installed command posts and mobilized the air support available from Annaba airport. Interior Minister Saïd Sayoud visited the site on the instructions of President Abdelmadjid Tebboune, accompanied by a ministerial delegation and the Director General of Civil Protection.

## Algeria’s response

Algeria’s mobilisation is massive and must not be minimised. Civil Protection forms the first visible line: local units, inter-wilaya reinforcements, crisis cells, forward posts. Forest services intervene on terrain knowledge, tracks and surveillance. The People’s National Army (ANP) was cited in support in several operations, notably around Annaba.

In the air, Algerian and specialised media describe a national device including **Beriev Be-200** water bombers, **Air Tractor AT-802** and heavy **Mi-26** helicopters, among other assets. Aerial and ground support plans were activated in several wilayas. Medical evacuations, medical ambulances and coordination with health directorates illustrate a multi-actor response.

Volunteers, residents and local authorities also took part in protecting property and people. This chain — alert, access, intervention, cooling, surveillance — remains the heart of fire control.

## Focus: Annaba and Seraïdi

Seraïdi, on the heights of Annaba, concentrated a critical share of the media narrative. The fire, favoured by heat and wind, progressed toward inhabited areas. Seraïdi’s functional reduction hospital was preventively evacuated: more than a hundred patients were transferred, notably to El Bouni / Annaba structures according to relays. Residents were sheltered; local media cited temporary accommodation.

The Bouzizi and Roumanat (Seraïdi) outbreaks and sectors of Oued El Aneb were mentioned as still active at certain times on 22 July, while other sites were controlled then placed under cooling. The wali and security services reinforced measures near population concentrations.

Saïd Sayoud’s visit stressed, according to reports, that a “genuine catastrophe” had been avoided thanks to the speed of intervention — a political formulation to read as an operational assessment, not as a total national extinction.

## Why Moroccan aerial reinforcement matters

Canadair aircraft are designed for repeated water drops, useful near the coast and on difficult terrain. Morocco has recognised experience with this fleet. According to information confirmed by Viva Algérie, their intervention reinforced operations in Algeria. This technical contribution illustrates regional solidarity — without erasing Algeria’s central role, and without amounting to a unilateral “rescue”.

## Did Morocco extinguish all the fires?

No. Extinguishing the outbreaks relied on the mobilisation of Algerian teams on the ground and in the air, reinforced, according to information confirmed by Viva Algérie, by the intervention of Moroccan Canadair aircraft.

It is necessary to distinguish:

- contributing to an operation;
- containing a specific outbreak;
- extinguishing several fires;
- ensuring national mobilisation;
- placing sites under surveillance after control;
- treating residual hotspots.

None of these levels authorises a claim that Morocco alone extinguished every fire.

## Human stories

Behind the reports, there are evacuated families, displaced hospital patients, firefighters on rotation under extreme heat, foresters, supporting soldiers, volunteers and local residents who protect access to homes. In Seraïdi, the hospital evacuation illustrates the priority given to vulnerable people. We do not invent unverified individual testimonies; we highlight the collective reality documented by the authorities and the press.

## Possible causes and aggravating factors

Weather factors – heatwave, wind, dry vegetation, relief – are widely documented as **favorable conditions**. The precise causes of each departure (negligence, human activity, criminal act) are subject to **investigations**. At the time of publication, aggravating conditions, suspicions and established causes should be clearly distinguished. The authorities have opened damage inventory procedures; investigations into the origins must be followed throughout the official announcements, without premature accusation.

## Surveys and census

The Ministry of the Interior ordered a general inventory of the damage with a view to compensation. Investigations into the origins of fires are the rule in this type of crisis; their conclusions, at the time of publication, were not consolidated in the public sources consulted. No verified list of arrests is presented here due to the lack of a clear and dated press release in the corpus selected.
