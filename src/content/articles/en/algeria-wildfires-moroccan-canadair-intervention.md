Major fires have affected several regions of Algeria in a context of intense heat, winds and dry vegetation. The Algerian authorities have mobilized land and air resources to protect the populations and control the outbreaks. According to direct information sent to the editorial staff of Viva Algérie, Moroccan Canadairs were also involved in the extinguishing operations. This information, however, remained awaiting official confirmation at the time of publication.

The human toll confirmed by the Ministry of the Interior, relayed in particular by AFP via Africanews on July 22, 2026, shows **six deaths** in the wake of the recent fires. Several wilayas in the north and other reported areas have recorded active outbreaks, while the General Directorate of Civil Protection (DGPC) has published hourly reports showing mass extinctions and fires still in progress. In Annaba, the commune of Seraïdi concentrated a critical part of the crisis, with hospital evacuations and material damage reported.

## What happened

Since the start of summer 2026, Algeria has been facing a particularly dense fire season. Data relayed by Anadolu Agency and CGTN, citing Civil Protection, indicate **3,719 fires** recorded between May 1 and July 20, 2026, compared to **1,501** over the same period in 2025 — an increase close to 150%. Since the beginning of July, approximately **1460** fire outbreaks have been cited.

The weather combination has made the situation worse: prolonged heatwave, hot winds, dry vegetation, difficult-to-access terrain. In several wilayas, forest, scrub, brush, harvest and sometimes palm grove fires required simultaneous interventions. Preventive evacuations were reported in several localities (Barbacha in Béjaïa, Blida, Skikda, Bouira, Guelma, among others), according to previous reports from mid-July.

In Annaba, a major fire broke out on Tuesday July 21–22 in the forests of Seraïdi before progressing towards urbanized areas. Local and national authorities have reinforced the ground presence, installed command posts and mobilized the air support available from Annaba airport. Interior Minister Saïd Sayoud visited the site on the instructions of President Abdelmadjid Tebboune, accompanied by a ministerial delegation and the Director General of Civil Protection.

## The Algerian response

The Algerian mobilization is massive and must not be minimized. Civil Protection constitutes the first visible line: local units, inter-wilaya reinforcements, crisis units, advanced posts. The forestry services (Forest Conservation) are involved in knowledge of the terrain, trails and surveillance. The National People's Army (ANP) was cited as support in several operations, notably around Annaba.

On the aerial level, the Algerian and specialized media describe a national device including water bombers of the **Beriev Be-200** type, **Air Tractor AT-802** and heavy helicopters **Mi-26**, in addition to other means. Air and land support plans have been activated in several wilayas. Medical evacuations, the deployment of medical ambulances and coordination with health departments illustrate a multi-actor response.

Volunteers, residents and local authorities also participated in the protection of property and people. This chain – alert, access, intervention, cooling, monitoring – remains the heart of fire control.

## Focus Annaba and Seraïdi

Seraïdi, on the heights of Annaba, concentrated a critical part of the media story. The fire, favored by the heat and the wind, progressed towards inhabited areas. The Seraïdi functional reduction hospital was the subject of a preventive evacuation: more than a hundred patients were transferred, in particular to structures in El Bouni / Annaba according to the relays. Residents were sheltered; local media cited temporary accommodation.

The outbreaks of Bouzizi and Roumanat (Seraïdi) as well as sectors of Oued El Aneb were mentioned as still active at certain times on July 22, while other sites were brought under control and then placed under cooling. The wali and the security services have reinforced the measures near population gatherings.

Said Sayoud's visit underlined, according to reports, that a "real catastrophe" had been avoided thanks to the speed of intervention - a political formulation to be read as an operational assessment, not as a total national extinction.

## Have Moroccan Canadairs intervened in Algeria?

**According to information directly provided to the Viva Algérie editorial team, Moroccan Canadair aircraft are reported to have taken part in the firefighting operations.**

The intervention had not yet been publicly confirmed by Algerian or Moroccan authorities at the time of publication.

What the editorial source indicates: participation of Moroccan Canadair type aircraft in extinguishing operations. What is **not** established in the public sources consulted for this article:

- aircraft registration number;
- departure/arrival airport;
- precise flight dates and times;
- exact locations of release;
- press release from the Algerian Ministry of the Interior explicitly mentioning Morocco;
- MAP press release / Moroccan ministry / FAR confirming a deployment in Algeria in July 2026;
- proof of independent geolocated and dated aerial tracking.

Searches in French, English, Spanish and Arabic did not make it possible, at the time of publication, to identify an official confirmation or independent verifiable visual proof for July 2026. Previous episodes (notably 2021) recall that Morocco had already offered Canadair to Algeria; this is **not** evidence of current intervention.

## Why Moroccan air aid would count (conditional)

If the information were to be confirmed, a contribution from Moroccan Canadairs would make operational sense: these amphibious aircraft are designed for repeated drops, useful near the coast and on difficult terrain. Morocco has recognized experience with this type of fleet. Such technical cooperation would illustrate local solidarity despite structural diplomatic tensions.

This reading remains **conditional**. It presupposes neither official diplomatic reversal, nor substitution of Algerian means, nor unilateral “rescue”.

## Has Morocco put out all the fires?

No. Even if the reported intervention by Moroccan Canadairs were to be confirmed, extinguishing the fires would result from a broader mobilization involving Algerian resources, local emergency services, ground teams, air resources and possible external reinforcements.

It is necessary to distinguish:

- contribute to an operation;
- master a specific focus;
- put out several fires;
- ensure national mobilization;
- place sites under surveillance after control;
- treat residual hot spots.

None of these levels authorizes a formulation such as “Morocco has turned everything off” without multiple, official and independent proofs – proofs of which we do not have.

## Human stories

Behind the reports, there are evacuated families, displaced hospital patients, firefighters on rotation under extreme heat, foresters, supporting soldiers, volunteers and local residents who protect access to homes. In Seraïdi, the hospital evacuation illustrates the priority given to vulnerable people. We do not invent unverified individual testimonies; we highlight the collective reality documented by the authorities and the press.

## Possible causes and aggravating factors

Weather factors – heatwave, wind, dry vegetation, relief – are widely documented as **favorable conditions**. The precise causes of each departure (negligence, human activity, criminal act) are subject to **investigations**. At the time of publication, aggravating conditions, suspicions and established causes should be clearly distinguished. The authorities have opened damage inventory procedures; investigations into the origins must be followed throughout the official announcements, without premature accusation.

## Surveys and census

The Ministry of the Interior ordered a general inventory of the damage with a view to compensation. Investigations into the origins of fires are the rule in this type of crisis; their conclusions, at the time of publication, were not consolidated in the public sources consulted. No verified list of arrests is presented here due to the lack of a clear and dated press release in the corpus selected.

## Regional solidarity

Fires know no diplomatic boundaries. Technical assistance between neighbors, when requested, accepted and documented, can save lives and forests. The possible Moroccan contribution reported to Viva Algérie is part of this humanitarian logic — **if** it is confirmed. It must neither erase Algerian work, nor serve as a polemical lever. ## FAQ

In several northern wilayas and other reported areas, with a particularly critical outbreak in Annaba/Seraïdi, and interventions notably in Skikda, Béjaïa, Guelma, El Tarf, Souk Ahras, Jijel, Bouira, Médéa, Mascara, among others.

Six deaths were announced by the Ministry of the Interior on July 22, 2026, according to AFP/Africanews dispatches.

DGPC reports vary depending on the time. The windows cited, for example, indicate 104 fires extinguished for 66 active, or 142 extinguished for 53 active over 24 hours. You must always relate the figure to its time window.

### Are the fires completely under control?
No in general: outbreaks remained active or under surveillance according to the latest reports consulted. The situation is evolving hour by hour.

According to direct information transmitted to Viva Algérie, yes. This information was not officially confirmed at the time of publication.

No, at the time of publication.

No. Even in the event of subsequent confirmation of a contribution, the extinction is the result of a broader mobilization, mainly Algerian.

Civil protection, foresters, ANP support cited, national air assets (notably Be-200, AT-802, heavy helicopters according to the press), evacuations and crisis units.
