> Editorial update: research completed on 21 July 2026 at 14:35 (Africa/Casablanca).

European demand for Algerian gas is back in focus because the European Union is continuing its Russian gas phase-out while remaining exposed to global LNG markets. Algeria is not a full replacement for Russia, but it remains an important regional supplier, especially through pipeline gas to southern Europe.

<div class="key-facts"><h2>Key points</h2><ul><li>Eurostat places Algeria as the EU s second supplier of gaseous natural gas in Q1 2026, behind Norway.</li><li>ACER says the Russian gas phase-out requires diversification without removing price risk.</li><li>Medgaz remains strategic for Spain, but capacity is limited.</li></ul></div>

## Why Algeria matters

European interest in Algeria rests on three factors: geographic proximity, existing pipelines and contract experience with European clients. That does not turn the country into a miracle supplier. A credible reading must hold together Algerian strengths and the physical limits of an energy system that depends on investment, maintenance and contractual predictability. See also [Algerian gas](/en/articles/algerie-energie-souverainete-gaz), [economy](/en/economy), [analysis](/en/analysis).

## Europe between diversification and prices

The European Commission and the Council have set a gradual path away from Russian imports. That path does not remove exposure to global markets: it shifts it towards LNG competition, spot prices, maritime tensions and storage seasons. Algeria can help diversification, but Europe will still need several suppliers.

## Medgaz as symbol and constraint

Medgaz is a symbol because it connects Algeria directly to Spain. It is also a constraint because a pipeline has fixed capacity. Serious energy diplomacy therefore speaks about realistic additional volumes, contracts and operational security, not slogans about total replacement of Russian gas.

## FAQ

### Can Algeria replace all Russian gas?
No. It can contribute to European diversification, but capacity and contracts limit any total substitution.

### Why is Medgaz important?
Because it links Algeria directly to Spain and reduces some transit risks.

### Does LNG solve everything?
No. It diversifies sources but exposes Europe more to global prices and maritime tensions.
