<div class="transparency-note"><strong>Geopolitical notice.</strong> Western Sahara’s final status remains unresolved and the UN still lists it as a Non-Self-Governing Territory. This article attributes every position and does not turn bilateral support into a United Nations ruling.</div>

<div class="key-facts"><strong>Quick verdict</strong><p>Morocco has broadened support for autonomy and won unprecedented positions from Washington and Paris. Security Council Resolution 2797 strengthens autonomy as the negotiating basis. It also retains a mutually acceptable solution providing for self-determination; it is not UN recognition of Moroccan sovereignty.</p></div>

## What the UN actually decided

Adopted on **31 October 2025** by 11 votes to none, with three abstentions, <a href="https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4093660?v=%5B%27pdf%27%5D">Resolution 2797</a> extends MINURSO to **31 October 2026**. It backs negotiations taking Morocco’s autonomy proposal as a basis, seeking a just, lasting and mutually acceptable solution that provides for the self-determination of Western Sahara’s people.

That language favours Morocco’s method but does not legally close the file. MINURSO remains and the UN process continues.

## Has Morocco gained ground?

Yes, diplomatically. Consulates opened in Laayoune and Dakhla by African, Arab and Caribbean states make bilateral support visible. US, French and Spanish positions carry more weight. France now places the territory’s present and future within Moroccan sovereignty and calls autonomy the only basis, going further than Spain.

Yet a consulate is one state’s act. It does not automatically reclassify a territory at the UN. Support counts also vary depending on whether they measure sovereignty recognition, backing for autonomy or merely favourable wording.

Algeria hosts refugee camps near Tindouf and supports Polisario. It says it defends decolonisation and is not a party; Rabat calls its role decisive. UN diplomacy consults Morocco, Polisario, Algeria and Mauritania without erasing the difference between parties and neighbours.

SADR membership of the African Union preserves an institutional counterweight. Morocco’s 2017 return to the AU did not remove SADR; it moved the contest inside the organisation.

## The recent changes that matter

Resolution 2797 is the central multilateral change: autonomy is expressly the negotiating basis, while self-determination and mutual acceptability remain. In 2026 the EU said genuine autonomy could be the most feasible outcome, still under UN leadership. Staffan de Mistura’s consultations resumed, but no final agreement had been announced by 5 September 2026.

## Who is ahead, and on which scoreboard?

Rabat leads in **support for autonomy** and bilateral gestures. Polisario and Algiers retain leverage in the **decolonisation framework**, AU membership and self-determination principle. “Final victory” is false in either direction. A genuine shift would require an accepted agreement or clear multilateral decision, not a pile of communiqués.

## FAQ

### Has the UN recognised Moroccan sovereignty?

No. Resolution 2797 takes autonomy as a negotiating basis but still seeks a mutually acceptable solution providing for self-determination.

### Does MINURSO still exist?

Yes. Its mandate runs to 31 October 2026.

### Do France, Spain and the US hold the same position?

No. Washington and Paris go as far as Moroccan sovereignty in their bilateral positions. Spain supports autonomy as the most serious, credible and realistic basis within the UN process.
