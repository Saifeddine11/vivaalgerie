> Editorial update: research completed on 21 July 2026 at 14:45 (Africa/Casablanca), during the announced BMS validity window through Tuesday.

The heatwave affecting several Algerian provinces should not be read as a simple weather episode. It creates a chain of risks: health, fires, pressure on power networks, emergency-service fatigue and vulnerability in rural or forest areas.

<div class="key-facts"><h2>Key points</h2><ul><li>The APS/Radio Algérienne BMS reported up to 47-48 C in several provinces.</li><li>Civil Protection reported 913 fires extinguished since 8 July, according to APS.</li><li>Fire counts must remain tied to their official publication window.</li></ul></div>

## Most exposed provinces

The bulletins distinguish red and orange levels by area. Relizane, Chlef, Ain Defla, Tizi Ouzou, Guelma, Skikda, Annaba, El Tarf, Bejaia and Jijel are among the provinces cited in the latest alert. This geography must be updated as soon as a new BMS replaces the previous one.

## Fires and prevention

Higher temperatures increase the sensitivity of forests, crops and peri-urban areas. Civil Protection figures are useful, but they should not be projected beyond their publication window. The editorial priority is prevention: avoid open fires, report fire starts, protect elderly people and limit exposure at the hottest hours.

## Power network and household safety

Heat increases air-conditioning use and can stress weak installations. Household safety therefore matters as much as weather reading: do not overload sockets, monitor appliances, ventilate and maintain equipment. Local outages should be confirmed by an operator or authority before publication.

## What to watch

Verify first: new Meteo Algeria BMS bulletins, DGPC reports, Sonelgaz announcements and provincial decisions on forest access or public events.

## FAQ

### Are the alerts still valid?
This article cites the announced validity through Tuesday 21 July 2026. Any newer bulletin takes priority.

### Can heatwave and outages be automatically linked?
No. Electricity demand rises, but each outage must be locally confirmed.

### Can fire figures be accumulated freely?
Only within the exact frame published by Civil Protection.
